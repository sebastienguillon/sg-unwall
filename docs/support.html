When a wall gets through.

Start with the checks below. Sites redesign their consent walls often, and a new one can slip past SG Unwall until it learns to recognise it.

Report a problem

Open an issue on GitHub with the page address and what you see. Issues are public: do not include personal information.

Quick checks

A wall is still on the page

Open SG Unwall’s popup from the toolbar and press Zap now: it hides whatever still covers the page and unlocks scrolling. If that works, report the page, so that SG Unwall learns to do it on its own.

Something I opened disappeared

SG Unwall leaves alone what you open with a click, but it can misjudge a window that takes a long time to appear. Press Undo on this page in the popup, or switch SG Unwall off for the site, then report the page.

The page still will not scroll

Press Zap now. Some sites block scrolling with scripts that cancel the mouse wheel or touch gestures; SG Unwall cannot undo those yet.

I want to give my consent on a site

Click the site’s own cookie settings link, usually at the bottom of the page: SG Unwall steps aside and the site’s dialog comes back. You can also switch SG Unwall off for that site from its popup.

The article is still cut short

SG Unwall removes what stands in front of a page, not content the site has left out. Articles reserved for subscribers stay reserved.

SG Unwall does nothing on a page

SG Unwall runs only on web pages: not on the browser’s own pages, on extension stores, or on files opened from your computer, and not inside frames embedded in a page. Check in its popup that it is active for the site.

When reporting a problem

  • The page address.
  • Your browser and its version, and SG Unwall’s version from the browser’s extensions page.
  • What you expected to happen, and what happened instead.
  • The list in SG Unwall’s popup, which says what it hid on the page and why.

Never include passwords, cookies or personal information. For how SG Unwall handles data, read the privacy policy.
